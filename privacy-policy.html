Privacy Policy — One Lane Left

Effective date: 15 September 2026

1. Introduction

One Lane Left is published by Dan-Mihai Balean ("we", "us", or "our"). This Privacy Policy explains how information is processed when you use One Lane Left on Android under package com.baleandanmihai.onelaneleft.

One Lane Left is a game. We do not provide player accounts or require you to log in to play. The game saves some game and settings information on your device. Online services may process information for consent management, analytics, advertising, or purchases when the relevant feature is used or enabled.

2. Information We Process

Information stored locally by the game

The game stores the following information on your device using app-local storage:

  • game progress such as high score, virtual currency total, and daily-mission state;
  • game settings, including sound, music, haptics, graphics, camera-motion, and usage-analytics choices;
  • a local flag recording a confirmed Remove Ads entitlement;
  • local records used to prevent the same validated purchase from being delivered twice; and
  • a locally generated random installation identifier used by the daily-mission save system. The game does not submit this daily-mission identifier as a custom analytics field.

This information is used to operate the game, restore local gameplay state, apply your settings, and protect purchase fulfillment. It is not a player account and does not contain a player name, email address, contacts, or payment-card information. Third-party SDKs may use their own installation or device identifiers as described below; those are separate from the game's local daily-mission identifier.

One Lane Left does not request Android precise-location, approximate/coarse-location, or background-location permissions and does not call Android device-location APIs. It also does not request access to contacts, photos or videos, audio files, health or fitness data, calendar data, messages, or direct payment-card credentials. Service providers may still infer approximate location, such as a country or general area, from an IP address.

Usage analytics

The game includes Unity Analytics. The game initializes Unity Services for Analytics and submits Analytics data only if you explicitly grant the separate Usage Analytics choice. If Usage Analytics is declined or remains unset, project analytics submission remains off.

When enabled, the game submits custom gameplay and technical events such as app readiness, app foreground/background state, screen views, button actions, run number, run duration, distance, score, virtual Cash, gameplay outcome, selected graphics tier, and performance measurements. These events include app version and build information. The game does not intentionally place your name, email address, contacts, precise location, raw store receipt, payment-card information, or raw purchase token into its custom analytics events.

Unity Analytics may also automatically process service data such as IP address, an installation-specific identifier, a service user identifier that defaults to the installation identifier, device and app information, diagnostics, performance information, and approximate location derived from IP address. This information is processed by Unity Technologies according to its applicable terms and privacy notice.

The project contains a bounded local crash-context metadata writer for technical game state. Unity's built-in Developer Data Diagnostics is enabled at project level and may transmit essential crash logs, exception and diagnostic information, other app-performance data, configuration metadata, and a unique installation-specific identifier for stability and service-integrity purposes. This diagnostic collection is managed at the project or build level, not by the Usage Analytics choice or UnityConsent. The legacy Unity Cloud Diagnostics service is not enabled.

The current Unity Developer Data project configuration is limited to the Essential Data collection tier and use by the Unity products and services used for this project. Additional Recommended Data is disabled. No Google Analytics or Firebase link, or additional Unity product access, is enabled.

3. Advertising and Advertising Identifiers

The direct LevelPlay mediation and bidding providers for the initial release are Google bidding/AdMob and ironSource bidding. The game creates ads through LevelPlay; it does not create a separate direct AdMob ad placement. Unity Ads is not connected to either mediation group and is not configured to request or deliver ads for this release.

The supported Unity LevelPlay package nevertheless bundles and manages the Unity Ads SDK and adapter. On Android, that bundled SDK registers a process-start component that performs limited SDK and networking-provider setup even when Unity Ads is not configured as a mediation source. One Lane Left does not call the Unity Ads initialization, load, or show APIs. Unity Ads therefore remains disclosed as a bundled technical component, but it is not an active direct advertising provider for this release.

When advertising is enabled and an ad request is permitted, these SDKs and the configured advertising partners may collect or share technical and advertising information. Depending on the provider and your choices, this can include an advertising identifier, other device or app identifiers, device and network information, IP address, approximate location inferred from IP address, app launch and ad interactions such as taps and video views, diagnostics, performance data, ad-delivery and measurement data, fraud-prevention signals, and consent or privacy-choice signals.

This information may be used to deliver, measure, limit, secure, and improve ads, prevent fraud, and personalize ads where permitted by your choices and applicable requirements. Dan-Mihai Balean does not use the advertising identifier as a first-party gameplay identifier.

4. Consent and Privacy Choices

The game has a separate Usage Analytics choice in its first-run and Settings experience. That choice sets Unity's Developer Data AnalyticsIntent and controls project analytics submission. Unity IAP can read and include that intent in its service telemetry. The choice does not authorize advertising and does not replace the separate UMP advertising-privacy flow.

On Android, the game uses Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP) to manage advertising privacy choices. At each app launch, including while live ad requests are disabled, the game asks UMP to refresh the applicable consent status and determine whether a privacy message or privacy-options entry point is required. UMP may communicate with Google and store or update consent and privacy-choice signals for this purpose.

When UMP requires an initial form, the game asks UMP to load and show it. LevelPlay stays blocked until UMP reports that ads may be requested. If UMP requires an ongoing privacy-options entry point, the game exposes a Privacy Choices control in Settings so that the UMP form can be opened again. If an update fails, the game does not infer consent; it relies only on the status returned by UMP, including any valid status retained by UMP from an earlier session.

The privacy message shown and the choices available depend on your region, the applicable requirements, and the advertising partners configured for the game. Google's UMP consent and privacy-options interface may list additional Google-certified advertising technology providers, Authorized Buyers, Open Bidding partners, or other Google demand sources that may be eligible to process data through Google's advertising ecosystem. The current partner list and each partner's disclosures are presented in that interface when applicable. Inclusion in that list describes eligibility or capability and does not mean that every listed provider receives data for every ad request.

Some UMP partner disclosures use generic language about precise geolocation because a listed provider has registered that capability. One Lane Left itself does not request Android location permission, obtain GPS-level location, or intentionally send precise location to an advertising SDK. This generic partner capability is not a statement that the game actually collects precise location.

5. In-App Purchases

The game uses Unity IAP 5.4 or later to offer Google Play purchases, including consumable virtual Cash packs and a non-consumable Remove Forced Ads product.

If purchases are activated, Google Play processes the payment and supplies purchase information to the app, and Unity IAP connects the game to Google Play Billing. Dan-Mihai Balean does not collect or store payment-card or bank-account numbers.

Unity states that IAP 5.4 and later processes personal data for the service. Depending on the available services and purchase flow, this may include a Unity installation identifier, an Authentication Player ID if Authentication is present, device information, session identifiers, country or approximate location, app and SDK information, product and purchase history, transaction identifiers or store payload data, diagnostics, performance information, and device or other identifiers. Unity's service-level Google Play disclosure also lists an end-user email address as required ephemeral app-functionality data. The game does not request an email address, include a player-account or Unity Authentication flow, or configure a direct-to-consumer payment provider.

Unity IAP 5.4 or later does not provide its own player-consent interface. The game sends the Usage Analytics decision to Unity as the Developer Data AnalyticsIntent. Unity IAP reads that signal and, when analytics is enabled, can include an optional Analytics identifier. Denying or leaving Usage Analytics unset does not stop the essential transaction and service data needed to provide purchases.

Advertising privacy choices remain managed separately through UMP. The game does not infer a Unity Developer Data advertising-consent signal from UMP's aggregate CanRequestAds result. In the current integration, Unity IAP therefore does not access optional advertising or linked Google Analytics/Firebase identifiers that its SDK gates on an explicitly granted advertising intent.

For purchase fulfillment, the game stores a local entitlement or Cash delivery record together with a one-way hash of the validated purchase token to prevent repeated local delivery. Eligible Remove Forced Ads purchases can be restored with the same Google Play account. Consumed Cash purchases do not reconstruct a lost local balance or progression state after app data is cleared or the game is reinstalled.
